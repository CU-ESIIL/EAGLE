# NAIP <-> 3DEP coregistration: running notes

Append-only log of progress, findings and open questions. Newest entries at the bottom.
Sign convention: (dx, dy) = metres (east, north) by which the NAIP content is displaced relative to the lidar.

## 2026-10-08 - setup

- Plan: three test sites (flat cropland IA, closed-canopy forest PA, mixed Boulder CO), 1.5 km x 1.5 km, 1 m analysis grid in NAD83 UTM, several NAIP vintages per site against one 3DEP project.
- Code: `src/eagle_als/coreg/data.py` (NAIP via Planetary Computer, 3DEP via PDAL/EPT, rasterised to DSM/DTM/CHM/intensity), `src/eagle_als/coreg/register.py` (estimators). Fetch script: `scripts/naip/01_fetch_sites.py`.
- Datum check: USGS EPT is EPSG:3857 with `+nadgrids=@null`, so its lon/lat are NAD83 values labelled WGS84. We reproject with no datum shift to NAD83 UTM (verified to 1e-9 m against a manual transform). Reprojecting via a WGS84 UTM zone would be numerically identical, so no hidden ~1 m datum error is introduced by this repo's fetch path.
- NAIP STAC metadata (Planetary Computer) has only year, GSD, CRS and bbox. No frame, flight-line, or GPS/RTK information, so there is nothing to exploit there yet. Still to check: USDA FSA NAIP index/frame shapefiles.
- Boulder lidar (CO_DRCOG_1_2020) is about 28 pts/m2; 1.5 km window = 66M points, about 65 s to stream.
- Planned evaluation metrics: (1) independent-block spread of shifts, (2) triangle closure between NAIP vintages, (3) known-shift recovery by synthetically displacing the lidar, (4) agreement between independent feature families and between ground and canopy strata.

## 2026-10-08 - first method comparison (`02_compare_methods.py`, 320 m blocks, search +/-8 m)

Sites fetched: `crop_ia` (-93.30, 42.45, rural Iowa, lidar Apr 2020 QL2), `forest_pa` (Laurel Highlands PA, lidar Nov 2019-Mar 2020 leaf-off, QL2), `mixed_co` (Boulder, lidar 2020 QL1). NAIP vintages 2013-2023 each. (First Iowa site landed in a town, replaced.)

Methods: NCC of lidar intensity vs NAIP NIR; NCC of gradient magnitudes (CHM vs NAIP luminance); mutual information (CHM vs NIR); multi-channel linear regression ("synthetic image": lidar features -> NAIP bands, scored by R^2 over all shifts via FFT normal equations).

Findings:
- **Raw offsets are large.** Boulder NAIP vs lidar: |d| about 1.5-5 m depending on vintage (2013-2023); NAIP vintages differ from each other by 0.5-4 m. Trusting provided coordinates is not good enough for sub-metre work.
- **Boulder (mixed): all estimators are precise** (block-to-block MAD 0.2-0.5 m) **but disagree with each other by 2-3 m in dy** (regression about -1.7 m N; NCC/MI about -4 to -5 m N). Precision is not accuracy. Suspected causes: sun-shadow (NAIP shadows fall north of objects, lidar has none) and relief displacement (NAIP is orthorectified to bare earth, so tall things lean away from the frame nadir).
- Regression estimator reproduces NAIP-vs-NAIP differences well in Boulder (e.g. 2013-2015: lidar-implied (-2.95,-0.24) vs direct NAIP-NAIP (-2.96,-0.24)), so inter-vintage consistency is a usable validation.
- **Cropland (Iowa) fails with 320 m independent blocks**: MAD 2-5 m, vintages inconsistent, even though NAIP vintages agree with each other to <1 m. Too few features per block; lidar was bare-soil April vs NAIP summer crops. Needs pooling over large extent and/or edge features.
- **Forest (PA)**: leaf-off lidar vs leaf-on NAIP; intensity feature is useless (score about 0.05); edge/regression give consistent dy (-0.3..-1) but dx varies by vintage (-4..+6 m, including 2022 hitting the search limit): search radius must be adaptive.
- Next: pool evidence across blocks; model lean + shadows; check how the shift varies at large scale (whole NAIP scene / lidar project); closure-based evaluation after correction.

## 2026-10-08 - shadows and lean are the accuracy problem (not noise)

- Added pooled estimation (`pool_surfaces`: mean of block score surfaces), `evaluate.py` (closure), `03_closure_eval.py`. Pooling fixes block noise but not bias.
- Cropland: raw-value matching fails (lidar April bare soil vs NAIP summer crops: R^2 about 0.06). Gradient-magnitude NCC of lidar intensity vs NAIP luminance/NIR over the whole 1.5 km window works (score 0.4-0.5, offsets clustered within about +-2 m across vintages), edges of roads/field boundaries/farmsteads carry the information.
- Boulder, height-stratified regression: ground-level pixels give dy of -4 to -7 m, while pixels under 3-8 m and >8 m objects agree with each other (dy about 0). The ground result is the **cast shadow**: shadows fall about h / tan(sun elevation) north of buildings/trees, are in NAIP but not in lidar, and drag the match south by metres. Naive "all pixel" estimates are therefore biased by 1-2 m in dy.
- Fix tested: lidar-derived shadow features (ray-march of the DSM for a sun azimuth/elevation, `features.shadow_features`). Scanning sun angles per vintage: R^2 0.223 -> 0.271 at az=120, el=50 for Boulder 2021 (plausible July morning sun), and the estimated shift moves from (+2.7,-0.8) to (+1.7,0.0). Sun angle is not in NAIP STAC metadata, so it is a fitted nuisance parameter.
- Vintage closure against direct NAIP-vs-NAIP offsets is **not** a clean truth: it was worse than naive for the plain regression (1.4 m vs naive 1.2 m at Boulder), because NAIP-vs-NAIP is dominated by ground features while regression sees tall objects, which have relief displacement that differs per vintage. Lean has to be modelled to compare.

## 2026-10-08 - sign bug fixed; shadows + lean model works at Boulder

- **Bug fixed:** `register._to_xy` returned the lidar-sampling offset rather than the NAIP displacement, so all (dx, dy) printed above this entry have the **wrong sign** (closure RMS and R^2 numbers are unaffected, since pairs flipped consistently). Verified now by displacing a real NAIP by a known (+3.0, -2.0) m and (-1.5, +2.5) m: recovered exactly (3.00, -2.00) and (-1.51, 2.54).
- Boulder with fitted sun, height-stratified (ground / 3-8 m / >8 m; old sign): ground-level estimates now agree with 3-8 m and >8 m ones to within the height-dependent lean. Strata dx differ systematically by vintage in sign and size (2013: -0.1/-0.7/-1.5, 2017: +0.3/+1.4/+2.4, 2019: -0.1/-1.5/-2.6, 2021: +1.0/+1.6/+2.3), dy hardly varies. That is relief displacement along the flight's cross-track direction: lean of about 0.1-0.25 m per m of height (a 20 m tree crown displaced 2-5 m relative to its base).
- Fitting (dx, dy, tx, ty) directly (`model.fit_geometry`, Nelder-Mead on regression R^2): lean tx = +0.12 (2013), -0.16 (2017), +0.24 (2019), -0.11 (2021), ty about 0, matching the strata analysis independently. Ground-plane shift after lean is small and consistent across vintages: all within about 0.7 m (e.g. 2013 (+0.2,-0.55), 2017 (-0.5,+0.7), 2019 (+0.5,+0.5), 2021 (-0.2,+0.4)), versus 1.5-5 m for plain matching on the same data.
- Written `coreg/api.py` (`estimate`, `apply`). 5 km windows being fetched (Iowa, PA done; Boulder in progress) to look at how the shift varies at large scale.

## 2026-10-08 - optimiser bias found; switched to stratified surfaces

- First lean implementation (Nelder-Mead on R^2 with interpolated lidar features) was **biased to x.5 m shifts** (fitted values clustered at +-0.5, 1.5, 2.5 in the Iowa 5 km run) and got stuck on the rough R^2 surface of weakly textured cropland. Known fractional-shift tests showed errors up to 0.4 m. Discarded as the main estimator.
- Replacement (`model.class_surfaces` + `fit_lean_model`): FFT regression R^2 surfaces per (250-300 m block, lidar height class: <0.5, 0.5-3, 3-8, 8-15, >15 m), then a joint fit of ground shift d0 and lean t such that class k appears at d0 + h_k t, using cubic-spline interpolation of the exact surfaces (no feature resampling => no interpolation bias). Block bootstrap gives reproducibility.
- Boulder agreement of the two independent lean estimators: tx = +0.12/-0.19/+0.27/-0.11 (stratified, 2013/17/19/21) vs +0.12/-0.16/+0.24/-0.11 (optimiser). Ground-plane shift agrees within about 0.5 m.
- Lean needs a prior: PA forest first returned tx = 0.50 (unphysical, sd 3 m); added a prior on |t| (< 0.35 hard-ish, quadratic below).
- Iowa 5 km: per-window shifts (old optimiser, so biased to x.5) still show large-scale structure: NAIP 2019 windows range -1.5..+1.5 m across the tile, 2013 -1.5..+2.5 m, and a few windows with no features (R^2 < 0.05) give garbage (-14, +12 m) and must be rejected by an R^2 / curvature threshold.

## 2026-10-08 - metadata findings (RTK / flight geometry question)

- **NAIP**: the Planetary Computer STAC item has no flight/frame/GPS info, but items from some vintages carry a `metadata` asset (FGDC .txt; 2019 Boulder has it, 2021 does not). The 2019 text states: Leica **ADS100 pushbroom** sensors (SH100/SH120, 4400/8400 m AGL, 12 km swath, nadir + forward + backward looks), 200 Hz GPS/INS exterior orientation per scan line, bundle adjustment (ORIMA) with photo-identifiable **GPS-surveyed ground control points**, orthorectified with the **June 2018 USGS 10 m NED DEM**, then mosaicked into DOQQs with OrthoVista; flight lines N/S unless E/W is more efficient. Accuracy spec since 2009: +-6 m to true ground. TIFF tags only say OrthoVista; no per-pixel frame or seamline info is distributed in the COG.
  - Consequence 1: a pushbroom has relief displacement **only cross-track** (lean along the line-normal; our fitted ty about 0 and tx of either sign on N/S lines fits this exactly) and lean grows with distance from the flight line: t = cross-track distance / AGL height, about 1.2e-4 per m at 8400 m, so across a 1.5 km window the lean changes by about 0.2 and across a 12 km swath by +-0.7, reset at OrthoVista seamlines.
  - Consequence 2: the orthorectification surface is the **10 m NED DEM**, not the lidar DTM. Displacement is (DSM - DEM10) * t, so where NED differs from the lidar ground (steep forest, pre-lidar NED) even bare ground leans. Not yet modelled (candidate extension: fetch the 3DEP 10 m DEM from Planetary Computer `3dep-seamless` and use DSM - DEM10 as the height in the lean term).
  - Consequence 3: GCP-adjusted bundle adjustment means that absolute NAIP error should be small (sub-metre to ~1-2 m); we do find 0-1.5 m ground-plane offsets in Boulder/Iowa, much less than the 6 m spec. RTK per se is not exposed; the GNSS base-station logistics are only described in text.
- **3DEP**: tile index gives project/workunit, QL, spec (Lidar Base Spec 2.1), horizontal CRS = NAD83(2011) UTM (EPSG 6342 CO, 6344 IA, 6346 PA), vertical NAVD88 (GEOID18/12B). Project reports (PDF) and QC text files are under `prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/metadata/<project>/<workunit>/reports/`; not parsed here. The points carry `PointSourceId` (flight line) and `GpsTime`, so a per-swath horizontal check is possible later (lidar swath-to-swath horizontal error is typically a few dm, i.e. an error floor for any NAIP alignment).
- NAIP datum: "NAD83" (realization not stated), lidar NAD83(2011). Realizations differ by up to a few dm in places; this is an unmodelled floor.

## 2026-10-08 - large-extent result (5 km x 5 km): the error is a smooth field, and the extent is what makes it estimable

Machinery: `04_shift_field.py` computes regression-R^2 surfaces for every 250 m block x height class over a 5 km grid (parallel); `model.SurfaceStack` makes sub-pixel lookups vectorised; `model.fit_field_model` fits a smooth model (ground shift a + A p, lean t0 + G p, p in km from the grid centre, h = lidar height) to all of them at once; `07_field_models.py` scores models out of sample with a 4-fold spatial checkerboard CV.

- **Iowa cropland (5 km, NAIP 2017 and 2019)**: a *constant* ground shift is enough. Affine = const in CV R^2 (0.1614 vs 0.1613; 0.2182 vs 0.2181). 2017 d=(-0.16,+0.12) m, 2019 d=(-0.64,+0.36) m. Window-to-window scatter of 0.36/0.23 m std matches bootstrap noise, i.e. the lidar-vs-NAIP error really is one tightly correlated offset over the area, which supports estimating it from the whole area instead of tile by tile. 3 of 16 windows had no usable structure (bootstrap sd > 1.5 m) and would have given garbage alone.
- **PA hilly forest (5 km, NAIP 2019)**: translation is *not* enough. Ground dx runs linearly from about -3 m (west edge) to +4.5 m (east edge) = about 1.4-1.8 m per km, while dy is constant at -0.04 +- 0.07 m. This is exactly the cross-track-only signature of a pushbroom orthorectification error (flight lines N/S, displacement along E-W). CV R^2: const 0.142, affine 0.230 (+0.09, held-out areas included). A single translation for the tile would be wrong by +-3-4 m at the edges; the affine field leaves 0.64 m windowed residual vs 2.6 m raw.
- Hypothesis for the PA gradient (not tested): displacement = (height error of the orthorectification surface) x cross-track lean; 1.4 m/km corresponds to about 6-12 m of height error at 4.4-8.4 km flying height, consistent with the 10 m NED DEM used for NAIP orthorectification being several metres off in steep terrain.
- `api.estimate_field` picks const vs affine automatically (affine only if held-out R^2 improves by >= 0.005).
